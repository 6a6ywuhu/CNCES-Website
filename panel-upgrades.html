What We Do

Panel upgrades and panel replacements

A panel upgrade adds capacity. A panel replacement swaps old equipment for new at the same size. We tell you which one your home needs.

100 to 200 amp service upgrades

For homes adding an EV charger, a heat pump or a suite. We size the new service from a load calculation, so you don't pay for capacity you won't use.

320 and 400 amp services

Upgrades from 200 amps to 320 or 400 amps for larger homes and estates with heavier loads.

Electrical panel replacement

Same-size replacement of an old, full or insurer-flagged panel, including fuse boxes and breaker boxes that are out of room.

Sub-panels

A sub-panel for a garage, suite or workshop, where your main service has room for it.

Before You Upgrade

Do you need a bigger service?

Not always. BC Hydro notes that adding a heat pump or an EV charger often doesn't require a service upgrade. A load calculation from your last 12 months of hourly meter data shows how much spare capacity you really have. You export the data from MyHydro and send it to us.

If you're close to the limit, an EV power management device or removing loads you no longer use can sometimes avoid the upgrade. If it can't, we size the new service for what you're adding now and what you have planned.

Insurance

Insurer asked you to replace a panel or wiring?

The Insurance Bureau of Canada lists knob and tube wiring, aluminum wiring and 60-amp services among the common risks in older homes, and notes that insurers may add surcharges or ask for proof the work was done. Ask your insurer exactly what they need, then we'll do the work and give you the permit details.

Federal Pioneer Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels

We replace the panel and its breakers with a new panel, and upgrade the service at the same time if your home needs more capacity.

Fuse box replacement and 60-amp services

A fuse panel replaced with a breaker panel, and a 60-amp service upgraded to the size your home needs today.

Knob and tube replacement

Knob and tube wiring has no ground wire, and its insulation can break down with age. We find which circuits still use it and replace them.

Aluminum wiring

Aluminum wire expands and contracts more than copper, which can loosen connections. We make each outlet and switch connection safer with copper pigtails.

How It Works

How a service upgrade works

  1. Load calculation

    We work out the service size you need from your meter data and the loads you're adding.

  2. Electrical permit

    We apply for the permit. In North Vancouver and West Vancouver, electrical permits are issued by the City of North Vancouver, the District of North Vancouver or West Vancouver, not Technical Safety BC.

  3. BC Hydro connection request

    BC Hydro needs you, the customer, to complete the connection application in MyHydro. We submit the contractor's declaration and the permit number.

  4. Approve BC Hydro's charges

    You approve BC Hydro's connection charges in MyHydro. BC Hydro sets these, not us.

  5. Upgrade day

    We install the new panel and service equipment, BC Hydro energizes the new connection, and we close out the permit.

Sources: BC Hydro: understand your home's electrical capacity; BC Hydro: how express connections work; Technical Safety BC: who issues electrical permits; Insurance Bureau of Canada: know your home's history.
